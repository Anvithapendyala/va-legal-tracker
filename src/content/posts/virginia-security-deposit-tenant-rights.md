## Status & Jurisdiction

Virginia Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (VRLTA), Va. Code § 55.1-1226. The statute applies to tenancies covered by the Act; some residential arrangements may fall under different rules. This summary is general information, not a substitute for reviewing the lease and current statute.

## The Legal & Financial Rule

When a covered tenancy ends, Virginia law generally requires the landlord to return the security deposit, or provide a written statement of deductions and the remaining balance, within 45 days after termination of the tenancy or the tenant's vacating the premises, whichever occurs last. Permitted deductions are limited by statute and may include unpaid rent and qualifying damages beyond ordinary wear and tear.

The landlord must provide an itemized written accounting when withholding money. The statute also addresses situations where the final amount cannot be determined within the initial period. Read the current [Va. Code § 55.1-1226](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title55.1/chapter12/section55.1-1226/) for the full requirements and exceptions.

## Why It Matters to Your Rights & Wallet

A deposit can be a significant part of a renter's move-in budget. Keep the lease, move-in and move-out photos, inspection notes, payment records, and written communications. Give the landlord a forwarding address in writing, review any deductions against the statute, and seek tenant legal aid if the accounting is late or disputed.
