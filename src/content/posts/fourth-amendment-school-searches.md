## Status & Jurisdiction

_New Jersey v. T.L.O._, 469 U.S. 325 (1985), is a binding U.S. Supreme Court decision for public schools nationwide, including Virginia. It concerns searches by school officials acting as school officials; different rules can apply when police conduct a search.

## The Legal & Financial Rule

After a teacher found a student smoking in a school restroom, a school administrator searched the student's purse and found evidence of other rule violations. The Supreme Court held that public-school officials do not need a warrant or probable cause for every school search. A search must be reasonable under the circumstances: it must have reasonable grounds at the start, and its scope must be reasonably related to its purpose and not excessively intrusive in light of the student's age and the suspected violation.

The case did not decide how every search of a modern smartphone should be handled. A phone can contain extensive personal information, and the reason for the search, school policy, state law, and who performs it all matter. The separate _Riley v. California_ rule for police searches of phones does not automatically govern a school administrator's search.

Sources: [_New Jersey v. T.L.O._](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/469/325/) · [_Riley v. California_](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/573/373/)

## Why It Matters to Your Rights & Wallet

Students and families can ask what prompted a search, what the school searched, and which policy applies. A search of a backpack is not automatically the same as a search through years of messages, photos, and account data. Write down what happened and seek advice promptly if school discipline or a police investigation follows.
