## Status & Jurisdiction

Virginia Code § 46.2-334.01 sets restrictions for provisional driver's licenses issued to people under 18. The limits generally end on the driver's 18th birthday. This summary describes the statute as of November 22, 2026; check the current text before relying on it.

## The Legal & Financial Rule

For a provisional-license holder under 18, the statute generally limits passengers under 21 to one. After the first year the license is issued, the driver may carry up to three passengers under 21 when driving to or from a school-sponsored activity, when accompanied by a licensed driver age 21 or older seated beside the driver, or in an emergency. The passenger limits do not apply to the family and household members defined in the law.

The statute also generally bars provisional drivers from driving between midnight and 4 a.m. Exceptions include driving to or from a job, a qualifying adult-supervised activity sponsored by a school or civic, religious, or public organization, driving with a qualifying parent, guardian-like adult, or adult spouse beside them, and emergencies. A violation is a traffic infraction. The statute also says police may not stop a vehicle solely to investigate a violation of these restrictions.

Read [Va. Code § 46.2-334.01](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title46.2/chapter3/section46.2-334.01/) for the full definitions, exceptions, and enforcement rules.

## Why It Matters to Your Rights & Wallet

Knowing the passenger and curfew rules can help families plan rides, work shifts, and school activities without an avoidable ticket or license suspension. Keep a copy of the current statute and ask a parent or guardian to confirm that a trip fits an exception; the details matter.
