## Status & Jurisdiction

The federal Credit Card Accountability Responsibility and Disclosure (CARD) Act of 2009 amended the Truth in Lending Act. Its credit-card protections apply nationwide, including to Virginia consumers. This is general information, not advice about a particular application or account.

## The Legal & Financial Rule

For an applicant under 21, a card issuer generally may not open a credit-card account unless the applicant can show an independent ability to make the required payments or a qualified cosigner age 21 or older agrees to be jointly liable. The law also restricts certain credit-card marketing on college campuses and at college-sponsored events.

The CARD Act directs that penalty fees be reasonable and proportional to the violation, subject to statutory and regulatory details. That is not a single, permanently fixed dollar cap for every fee. Rules and court decisions can change, and different fees may be governed by different provisions. See [15 U.S.C. § 1637](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1637) and [15 U.S.C. § 1665d](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1665d).

## Why It Matters to Your Rights & Wallet

An application that asks for a cosigner or proof of income may reflect federal age-based requirements, not just a bank preference. A cosigner can be responsible for the debt, so read the terms together before signing. Compare fees and statements, and do not assume a “penalty fee” or marketing offer is automatically lawful or unlawful without checking the current rule and facts.
