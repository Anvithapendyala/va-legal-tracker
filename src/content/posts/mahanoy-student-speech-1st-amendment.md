## Status & Jurisdiction

Decided by the U.S. Supreme Court in 2021, 8–1. The First Amendment ruling applies to public schools nationwide, including in Virginia.

## The Legal & Financial Rule

A high school student posted a frustrated Snapchat message off campus over the weekend after failing to make the varsity cheerleading squad. The school suspended her from the junior varsity team for a year, arguing her post disrupted school morale.

In _Mahanoy Area School District v. B.L._, the Court held that schools have diminished authority over off-campus speech, while recognizing that some circumstances—such as serious bullying or threats—may still justify school intervention. The decision does not create blanket immunity for all off-campus speech.

## Why It Matters to Your Rights & Wallet

Public schools in Virginia and nationwide face constitutional limits before punishing students for off-campus posts, group chats, or political opinions shared outside school events. The decision can help families evaluate a discipline policy, but its application depends on the speech and the school’s stated reasons.
