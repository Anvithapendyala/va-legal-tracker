## Status & Jurisdiction

_In re Gault_, 387 U.S. 1 (1967), is a U.S. Supreme Court decision applying the Fourteenth Amendment's Due Process Clause to juvenile delinquency proceedings. Its constitutional protections bind state courts, including Virginia courts, when a delinquency adjudication may result in confinement. The exact procedures depend on the case and current state law.

## The Legal & Financial Rule

The case involved 15-year-old Gerald Gault, who was committed to a state industrial school after a juvenile-court proceeding that lacked several safeguards expected in adult criminal court. The Supreme Court held that due process requires important protections in delinquency proceedings, including timely written notice of the specific allegations, assistance of counsel, an opportunity to confront and cross-examine witnesses, and protection against compelled self-incrimination.

These are not a declaration that juvenile court is identical to adult criminal court, nor do they apply in exactly the same way to every family, status-offense, or informal proceeding. The ruling focused on delinquency adjudications that can lead to loss of liberty. Read the [_In re Gault_ opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/387/1/) and the current Virginia rules for the specific proceeding.

## Why It Matters to Your Rights & Wallet

A juvenile case can affect a young person's freedom, education, and future opportunities. A notice of allegations is not a conviction, and a young person facing a delinquency charge should understand the accusation and available process before answering questions or agreeing to a resolution. Families can ask for counsel and consult a qualified juvenile-defense attorney.
