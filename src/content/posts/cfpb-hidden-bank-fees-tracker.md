## Status & Jurisdiction

Federal consumer-finance rules and enforcement actions are subject to ongoing administrative review and litigation. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) and Federal Trade Commission (FTC) have roles in different areas of consumer protection; the specific authority depends on the issue.

## The Legal & Financial Rule

For covered accounts, the federal Electronic Fund Transfer Act's Regulation E generally bars a bank from charging an overdraft fee for paying an ATM or one-time debit-card transaction unless the consumer has affirmatively opted in. The rule does not cover every transaction that can trigger an overdraft fee. See [12 C.F.R. § 1005.17](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1005/17/).

The Virginia Consumer Protection Act, Va. Code § 59.1-200, prohibits specified deceptive practices, but exemptions in § 59.1-199 may apply to some financial institutions. Whether either law applies depends on the provider, account, and transaction; an unexpected fee alone does not establish a violation.

Federal agencies and financial companies also litigate the scope of fee rules and regulatory authority. Case status can change, so check the current rule and court record before relying on an older summary. The [Virginia Code](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title59.1/chapter17.8/section59.1-200/) provides the text of the state consumer-protection provision.

## Why It Matters to Your Rights & Wallet

Review the account agreement and opt-in choice for ATM or one-time debit-card overdraft coverage. Keep the transaction record, fee notice, and bank response if you dispute a charge. This tracker is general information, not legal or financial advice.
