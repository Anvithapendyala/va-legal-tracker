## Status & Jurisdiction

This briefing covers federal consumer-credit guidance for Buy Now, Pay Later (BNPL) products. In May 2024, the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) issued an interpretive rule addressing certain BNPL lenders under the Truth in Lending Act and Regulation Z. The CFPB withdrew that interpretive rule in 2025. As of November 23, 2026, do not treat the withdrawn guidance itself as a current, binding source of credit-card-style rights.

## What Happened / The Legal Rule

The 2024 interpretation said that certain BNPL providers extending closed-end credit could qualify as “card issuers” under Regulation Z for parts of their products. That interpretation described billing-dispute, refund, and related protections similar to those for credit cards. In May 2025, the CFPB published a notice withdrawing the 2024 BNPL interpretation along with other guidance documents; consumers should not assume those protections apply to every BNPL plan based only on the withdrawn rule.

That does not mean every BNPL transaction is outside federal law. The product structure, other Truth in Lending Act requirements, the Fair Credit Reporting Act, state law, and the actual contract can still matter. The Federal Register includes the CFPB's [2024 interpretive rule](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/05/31/2024-11864/truth-in-lending-regulation-z-buy-now-pay-later-bnpl-interpretive-rule) and its [May 2025 withdrawal notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/05/12/2025-08286/interpretive-rules-policy-statements-and-advisory-opinions-withdrawal). Check current agency materials and the provider's terms before relying on any particular dispute or refund right.

## Why It Matters to Your Rights & Wallet

Save the checkout disclosure, installment schedule, payment receipts, and any messages about a return or billing error. If a purchase is returned, separately confirm that the merchant processed the refund and the lender adjusted the balance. If a credit report contains inaccurate BNPL information, you can dispute the specific error with the reporting company under the Fair Credit Reporting Act.
