## Status & Jurisdiction

Virginia's Disposition of Unclaimed Property Act is in Va. Code Title 55.1, Chapter 25. Section 55.1-2500 defines terms, including intangible property; § 55.1-2501 supplies a general five-year presumption of abandonment, subject to exceptions and special rules elsewhere in the chapter.

## What Happened / The Legal Rule

The Act covers certain financial property held or owed by businesses and institutions. The statutory definition of intangible property includes items such as refunds, customer overpayments, unpaid wages, deposits, and other amounts. Under the general rule, property that remains unclaimed for more than five years after it becomes payable is presumed abandoned, unless another provision sets a different period or condition.

Holders report qualifying property to the state, which acts as custodian so an owner or heir can make a claim. A business's transfer of property to the state does not mean the owner has forfeited it. Use the Virginia Treasury's official [unclaimed property search](https://trs.virginia.gov/Unclaimed-Property/) and follow its claim instructions; do not pay an unsolicited caller to search the public database for you.

Read [Va. Code § 55.1-2500](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title55.1/chapter25/section55.1-2500/) and [§ 55.1-2501](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title55.1/chapter25/section55.1-2501/). Dormancy periods vary by property type.

## Why It Matters to Your Rights & Wallet

Young adults may have money waiting from a prior job, an old utility account, a closed bank account, or an overpayment. Search using your current and former names and addresses, then submit documentation only through the state Treasurer's official process. Keep records of former employers and accounts so a match can be verified.
